# Regional shirts and historical motif-construction records

Expanded research beyond the previous towels and three shirts: read 13 additional Krovets object records and visually examined all 13 public title previews, covering ten documented ethnographic regions. Twelve are women's shirts and one is a men's shirt. The sample uses the first public listing in each regional category plus three deliberate comparisons; it is not representative enough to define universal regional styles. Also examined five historical drawing/instruction sources and selected sections of two Ukrainian scholarly publications. Source hashes, catalogue fields, observations, review scope and bibliographic leads are retained in data/research/motif-annotations/regional-shirt-and-historic-drawing-study.json.

### Connecting Vytvory to the original record

The previously examined [Vytvory product 278](https://vytvory.ua/uk/product/sorochka-zhinocha-traditsiyna-278) states that its embroidery uses the example of shirt KYD58. The [Krovets KYD58 record](https://krovets.ua/item/kro-340) provides Chernihiv Polissia attribution, an early twentieth-century date, geometric classification, a white palette and named techniques. This gives an object-specific documentary connection. It does not establish that the modern product's colour/presentation is identical to the historical shirt, or justify transferring that identity to the unrelated Neon plates.

These sources demonstrate multiple construction systems. They do not demonstrate that Ukrainian artists used the same books, that identical-looking motifs share a historical origin, or that their work followed mathematical fractals or Penrose rules.

### Ukrainian scholarly and bibliographic evidence

Read selected design-analysis, modelling and construction sections of [T. S. Machacha, Ukrainian Folk Embroidery](https://doi.org/10.32405/978-966-644-495-3-2019-135), including printed pp.69–74. Its sequence compares cultural analogues, produces alternative sketches, determines ornament size/position, reviews alternatives, and then develops technical drawings, material choices and operation plans. This supports keeping source analysis, visual composition and manufacturing planning as separate stages. The PDF's bibliographic line gives 2017, while copyright and DOI metadata give 2019; both are retained.

Read the abstract, selected source-evidence/origin passages, conclusions and bibliography of [Vasyl Balushok and Maryna Oliynyk, Embroidery on Ukrainian Clothes: the Problem of Origins, 2021](https://doi.org/10.15407/nz2021.05.1150). It explicitly presents unresolved questions and hypothetical directions for early origins. Its discussion is not evidence that a particular new flower has prehistoric symbolism, or that an entire motif family has been historically verified.

The bibliographies identify particularly useful further records:

- **Ya. Klunny and H. Klunny, Album of Ukrainian Patterns: satin stitch, cutwork, drawn-thread work, Kyiv, 1915, 48 pp.** Cited in Machacha, PDF p.133, item 10. This is a priority historical drawing/chart lead.
- **P. Savitsky, On Ukrainian Embroidery of the Eighteenth Century and Its Contemporary Revival, 1914**, Chernihiv Zemstvo Week, no.24, pp.1–5. Cited in Balushok/Oliynyk, PDF p.12, item 5. Useful lead on historical reconstruction/revival.
- **A. Miller, Lotus in the historically named “Little Russian” Ornament, 1911, pp.81–88.** Cited in the same paper, PDF p.14, item 42. Historical terminology is retained only to identify the source; proposed botanical derivations have not been independently verified.
- **M. R. Selivachov, Lexicon of Ukrainian Ornamentation, 2005.** A lead for iconography, naming, stylistics and typology, rather than guessing names from shape.
- **Tetiana and Yevhen Prychepii, Embroidery of Eastern Podillia, 2008.** A regional analysis lead cited in Machacha, PDF p.135, item 30.
- **L. Pokusinska and O. Pokusinskyi, Borshchiv Folk Shirt: Materials, Cut, Stitching Techniques, 2012, 368 pp.** A specific garment/construction lead cited in Machacha, PDF p.135, item 28.

These six are verified bibliographic leads in retrieved publications, **not originals read**. Archive searches did not locate the Klunny album or the Pchilka/Kosach ornament publication in this session. Several other discovery endpoints were blocked or rate-limited; their unavailable pages are not used as evidence.

## Construction approaches worth separating in Tesseract

1. **Radial blossom construction:** establish centre and outer mass, then subdivide into petal/lobe groups, subordinate centres and interior texture. Derive these from annotated sources; do not assume a universal petal count.
2. **Axial plant and leaf construction:** establish stem or midrib, then branch/vein tiers and terminal attachments. The skeleton and the contour have different roles. Preserve explicit ports and clearance between adjacent leaves/flowers.
3. **Counted geometric construction:** establish warp/weft grid, nested shapes and repeat units. Keep unequal measured fabric pitches; pixels or chart squares are not an automatic stitch count.
4. **Shape-constrained repetition:** define the garment/panel boundary first, then field network, repeat size, border and corner/terminal treatments. Existing rectangular layout helpers are a baseline; shaped panels, seams and gathers remain incomplete.
5. **Whitework and cutwork construction:** model fabric voids and bridges, relief/surface stitches and drawn-thread structures separately. A silhouette mask or run-stitch outline cannot stand in for the complete operation.
6. **Interlace construction:** preserve strand continuity and over/under order at crossings. Existing MotifGraph junctions do not encode this order; declaring every visual crossing a junction would be wrong.
7. **Design-to-execution translation:** retain source annotation, composition sketch, calibrated working drawing, stitch plan and physical verification as separate evidence. Historical tracing/pouncing illustrates why an attractive drawing alone is not a complete embroidery execution plan.

These are research-derived implementation requirements, not newly trained grammars or completed engine features. Canonical ASCEND geometry remains unchanged. The strongest next permitted visual reconstruction target is a counted or radial/axial drawing with independently annotated outer form, subdivisions and attachments; exact machine output still requires physical calibration and selected machine constraints.
